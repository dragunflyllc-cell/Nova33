# Dragunfly — Monetization Plan

Every revenue stream below is free to set up (no upfront cost, standard free
developer/business accounts only).

## Phase 1 — Free, build an audience
No monetization yet. The goal is real pilots actually playing and posting
catch footage. Revenue depends on having players first.

## Phase 2 — Cosmetics (in-app purchases, no store needed)
- Stripe Checkout (free to integrate, ~2.9%+30¢ per transaction, no monthly fee)
  sells cosmetic skins: drone marker skins, capture-effect animations,
  profile badges. Nothing pay-to-win — altitude/GPS requirements never change.
- "Founder's Badge" one-time purchase for early adopters — also doubles as
  a launch marketing hook ("first 500 pilots get a permanent badge").

## Phase 3 — Sponsored locations
Once there's regional density of players, drone-friendly parks, drone
racing tracks, and tourism boards can sponsor a featured high-rarity spawn
at their location for a flat weekly/monthly fee — the Pokémon GO
"sponsored PokéStop" model. This is the highest-ceiling revenue line and
costs nothing to build (it's the existing spawn system with a manually
pinned location).

## Phase 4 — Affiliate revenue
- Amazon Associates (free to join) links on drone gear recommendations
  shown after a catch ("upgrade your setup").
- Drone insurance / training course affiliate partners.

## Phase 5 — Rewarded ads (optional, opt-in only)
A rewarded-video partner (e.g. AdMob web) offering an extra daily spawn
refresh in exchange for watching an ad. Kept optional so it never feels
required to compete.

## Phase 6 — Opt-in aerial data contribution
After a catch, players can optionally snap a ground photo (Data Contributor
mode, off by default). Today this only stores photos locally on the
player's own phone — there is no data buyer integrated and nothing is
uploaded automatically. This is scaffolding for a legitimate future
revenue line (companies like DroneDeploy/Propeller Aero already pay pilots
for aerial imagery), but it only ships as real monetization once there's
an actual data partner, explicit disclosure of who receives the data and
why, and a separate consent step distinct from this toggle. Never framed
as something incidental to gameplay — it's a clearly labeled, separate
mode a player has to deliberately turn on.

## What we deliberately avoid
- No pay-to-win altitude/GPS shortcuts — that would undermine the "you
  actually have to fly there" premise that makes this game credible to
  real pilots.
- No subscription paywall on core play — free-to-play is the growth engine.
